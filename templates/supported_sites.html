⚡ Quick Answer / Key Takeaway

What streaming websites are supported? Powered by the open-source yt-dlp core, YT Downloader supports public media extraction from YouTube (Standard videos, Shorts, and Playlists), Vimeo, SoundCloud, Bandcamp, Twitter/X, and Twitch Clips. We strictly do NOT support DRM-encrypted services (such as Netflix, Spotify, or Disney+) or paywalled content.

  • Public Streams Only: No login or password bypass required.
  • Zero DRM Circumvention: Pure adherence to open web media standards.
  • Batch Compatible: Queue URLs from any verified platform simultaneously.

Strict Limitations & What We Do NOT Support

In adherence to ethical software engineering and copyright protection laws:

  • DRM-Protected Services: We do NOT support and will never attempt to decrypt services protected by Widevine, FairPlay, or PlayReady DRM (e.g. Netflix, Amazon Prime, Spotify, Disney+, Hulu).
  • Paywalled or Private Content: Videos requiring paid subscription tiers, patron access, or account login credentials are not accessible.
  • Region-Blocked Content: We do not operate circumvention proxies or automated IP hopping to bypass platform geolocation restrictions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if a link from a supported site fails?

Streaming platforms occasionally update their video player cipher or API format. Our yt-dlp upstream dependency publishes regular updates to accommodate these changes. You can also inspect the URL in our Video Info Checker to see if metadata is readable.

Can I test multiple links at once?

Yes. Use our Bulk Audio or Bulk Video tools to paste batches of up to 20 URLs.

Why does YT Downloader reject links to private videos?

Private and unlisted videos requiring user account authentication cannot and should not be accessed without the creator's authorization. We do not accept user passwords or session cookies to access restricted content.

Can I suggest a new streaming site for testing?

Yes. You can contact our team through the Contact & Support page or submit extractor pull requests directly to the open-source yt-dlp project.
